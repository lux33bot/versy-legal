CHILD SAFETY STANDARDS

Draft — not yet in force

Draft — this page is not yet in force. It will be replaced by the final wording before launch.

Our standard

Versy has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation. Any content or behaviour that sexualises, exploits or endangers a child is removed, the account is closed, and we report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) as US law requires.

Not allowed

Child sexual abuse material of any kind, including drawn or generated images; sexualised content involving anyone under 18; grooming, sexual messages to or about minors, or asking a minor for images; sextortion; trafficking or offering minors; and links to any of these anywhere off Versy.

How Versy protects young people

Versy is for people 13 and older. Accounts under 18 are private by default, see a chronological feed, and can message only people they follow who follow them back. Someone you don’t follow back can send one text-only request; nothing more reaches you unless you accept. Text is filtered automatically when it is posted.

How we find it

Every photo and video is checked automatically against known child sexual abuse material before anyone else can see it, using Project Arachnid Shield from the Canadian Centre for Child Protection. Every entry and post goes into a review queue, and anything reported is looked at by a person; reports that involve a minor reach our moderators at once.

What we do

An upload that matches known material is blocked at once and the account suspended. Reported material is reviewed by a moderator; anything that breaks these standards is taken down and the account suspended. The material is kept securely as evidence for the period the law requires and reported to NCMEC’s CyberTipline. We cooperate with law enforcement and share information when the law requires it or when a child’s safety is at immediate risk.

How to report

In the app: on the entry, post, message or profile, tap ••• → Report → Involves a minor. Reports are confidential; the person you report is not told who reported them.

You can also report directly to NCMEC’s CyberTipline at missingkids.org. If a child is in immediate danger, call 911 or your local emergency number.
